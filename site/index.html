Every brochure, and what was pulled out of it

Each ANBG volunteer walk sheet puts the plants in flower in bold, usually followed by a garden bed reference like [Section 30]. The parser reads that convention, cleans the name, and resolves it against the Atlas of Living Australia so misspellings and renamed species (e.g. Bracteantha bracteata → Xerochrysum bracteatum) join up across 20 years. Pick a brochure to compare the extraction with the original page.

Which flowers are blooming earlier, and why?

The garden-wide signal

Flowering onset, all taxa combined

For each species, every season's first appearance in the brochures is compared with that species' own average. Averaging those differences across all species in a year gives a garden-wide onset index. Below zero means the garden flowered earlier than usual.

Which flowers are shifting

326 taxa appear in at least 8 well-sampled seasons spanning 10+ years. The shift is a linear trend in first-appearance date, in days per decade. The table opens sorted by strength of evidence (p). Very large shifts usually belong to long-flowering plants and are not reliable. Individual species' records are noisy, because a plant only shows up if the volunteer's walk that week passed it. So read single-species results as leads, and check the q value (false discovery rate across all 326 tests) before trusting one.
